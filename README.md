# wz-customs

Results, ranking review and promotion/demotion history for the Warzone Resurgence
customs series.

Live site: **https://slight-oce.github.io/wz-customs/**

The public site shows promotions, the near-misses that were considered and held, every
player's own numbers, and the ranking rule in full. **Demotions are not published.**
Someone being moved down is between them and the organisers — but the full reasoning is
one command away when they ask for it:

```bash
python scripts/build.py          # admin build -> admin/data.json (gitignored)
```

---

## Why it is built this way

The first tournament was analysed by hand off Discord screenshots. Two things
went wrong, and the whole repo layout is a response to them.

**Numbers were transcribed wrong and nothing caught it.** Fifteen player damage
figures were out by an average of 26%. They survived review because the only
thing they were checked against was a squad total read off the same image at the
same moment — so a misread player row and a misread total agreed with each other.
Kills never had that problem, because kills were independently checked against
the team totals in the Google Sheet.

The fix is `map_results.csv`. It stores the **SQUAD TOTALS row as its own record**,
entered separately from the player rows. `scripts/validate.py` then requires the
player rows to sum to it. Get a digit wrong and CI fails.

> Note: for the 19 Jul dataset the squad totals were derived from the player rows,
> so that check passes trivially. From the next event, type them in from the
> scoreboard separately — otherwise the check is circular and proves nothing.

**Conclusions were revised on data that was itself wrong.** One player's promotion
was withdrawn on a six-map figure where three of those maps were bad. Version
control is the answer: every number has a commit, every correction has a diff,
and any conclusion can be traced back to the data as it stood when it was drawn.

## Layout

```
data/
  players.csv        one row per person. player_id is the key, never the gamertag
  aliases.csv        every in-game name seen -> player_id
  ranks.csv          rank as a time series, so "what were they on the day" is answerable
  tournaments/<date>/
    meta.csv         date, format, map count
    scoring.csv      placement multipliers as used for THAT event
    squads.csv       rosters
    map_results.csv  one row per squad per map, incl. the squad totals row
    player_map.csv   one row per player per map. the atomic fact.
    decisions.csv    who moved, who didn't, why, and whether it's public
scripts/
  validate.py        integrity checks. exits 1 on error.
  build.py           CSV -> JSON. --public writes docs/, bare writes admin/
docs/
  index.html         the public site, served by GitHub Pages
  data.json          generated by `build.py --public`, do not hand-edit
admin/
  data.json          full build incl. demotions and residuals. GITIGNORED.
```

A gamertag is never a key. Three players changed name mid-tournament
(`@swanny`/Vgl, `subway worker`/Nicc!, `[M]selfish`/Rybug). If the tag were the
key, one rename would silently fork somebody's history.

## Pushing

`scripts/gh_push.py` sends the whole repo as **one commit** via the Git Data API, rather
than the Contents API's one-commit-per-file.

```bash
export GITHUB_TOKEN=github_pat_...
python scripts/gh_push.py --repo slight-oce/wz-customs -m "19 Jul results"
python scripts/gh_push.py --repo slight-oce/wz-customs --dry-run   # list files, send nothing
```

Token: fine-grained, **Contents: read and write on this repo only**, shortest expiry
offered. Revoke it once the push lands — the script says so on completion.

It reads `.gitignore`, so `admin/` never goes up.

## Adding a tournament

1. `cp -r data/tournaments/2026-07-19 data/tournaments/<new-date>` and clear the rows.
2. Fill `player_map.csv` and `map_results.csv` from the scoreboards. Put the
   Discord message link in `evidence_url` so anything can be re-checked later.
3. Add a `ranks.csv` row for anyone promoted or demoted since. `effective_on`
   must be **after** the tournament they earned it at, or they get compared to
   the band they just left.
4. `python scripts/validate.py` — fix errors, read the warnings.
5. Push. CI validates, rebuilds `data.json` and Pages updates.

## Public vs admin

`decisions.csv` has a `visibility` column. `public` reaches the site; anything else stays in
the admin build. `build.py --public` also strips the band residual from every player row —
that field is the one that orders people worst-to-best, and publishing it turns a rank
review into a shame list even without a demotion label attached.

`validate.py` checks the built `docs/data.json` for all three leaks: non-public decisions,
any demotion, the residual field, and discord IDs. It fails the build rather than warning.

## Confidence codes

| | |
|---|---|
| `C` | read off a squad scoreboard screenshot |
| `K` | kills confirmed, damage/assists/redeploys not captured |
| `T` | team total only from a text post, no per-player split |
| `L` | from the league leaderboard, no screenshot. A zero here means wiped early |
| `I` | imputed — team total known, split estimated from the player's share elsewhere |
| `U` | unverified |
| `DNP`| squad did not report |

Anything that is not `C` or `K` shows as unconfirmed on the dashboard.

## Reading the output

`ppm` is points per map: the player's own kills times their squad's placement
multiplier. `res` is the gap to the average of their own rank band — that is the
number that carries between tournaments, because a raw score depends on how
strong the lobby was.

A player's score for an event is the **median of their maps**, not the mean. Tanking the
back half of a night moves a mean a long way and a median hardly at all — 2.68 against
0.35 on a worked six-map example — so giving up is not a route to an easier rank.

The threshold is **1.5 standard deviations from the band, recomputed every event**, not a
fixed number of points. Band widths differ enormously: a flat +2.50 was 0.8 deviations in
rank 8 and 3.8 in rank 6, which made promotion out of rank 6 effectively impossible. Bands
with fewer than five players have no usable spread and move nobody automatically.

The flag only fires when someone clears the line **in the same direction at two consecutive
events**, in both directions.

### Brackets

`meta.csv` records `bracket`, `rank_cap` and `movement_floor`. A capped bracket does not
sample the low ranks fairly — an `8-` fills with 8s and 7s because those are the strongest
players eligible, and the few lower ranks who enter chose to play a field above themselves.
They are a self-selected group, not a sample of their rank, and more of them turning up
would not fix it. So the floor is structural, not a sample-size rule: for 19 Jul it is 6,
and ranks 4 and 5 do not move automatically regardless of how many attend.

Carry-forward should only chain events with the same `bracket`. A rank 8 residual from an
`8-` (where 8 is the top band) is not the same measurement as one from an open field.

### Promotion out of the cap is an exit

In an `8-` series, promoting someone to 9 removes them from it. That is the intended
direction — the bracket is for sub-9 players and the best graduate out — but it means a
promotion is a bigger act than a label change, and the two-event rule matters more than it
would in an open ladder.

### Known limitation

A wide band protects its weakest members. Rank 8 currently holds 22 of 47 players and spans
1.43 to 14.10, so its demotion line computes to 0.75 — low enough that nobody realistically
reaches it. Two players with an identical median of 0.50 came out differently for this
reason: one in tight rank 5 was flagged, one in loose rank 7 was not.

That is a symptom of the ladder being compressed at the top rather than a fault in the
maths. Promoting the clear rank 8 outliers upward is what fixes it. Until the bands tighten,
demotion out of a wide band stays a judgement call rather than an automatic flag.
